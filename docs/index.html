Membership Inference Attacks against Large Audio Language Models

Jia-Kai Dong1, Yu-Xiang Lin1, Hung-yi Lee1,2

1National Taiwan University 2NTU Artificial Intelligence Center of Research Excellence

Interspeech 2026

Paper arXiv Code Dataset

Abstract

We present the first systematic membership inference attack (MIA) evaluation of LALMs. Using Multi-modal Blind Baselines based on textual, spectral and prosodic features, we demonstrate that common audio datasets exhibit near-perfect train/test separability (AUC ≈ 1.0) even without model inference, thus MIA may primarily detect distribution shift. We therefore introduce a blind-baseline protocol to control for this confound. Under this protocol, we identify that the distribution-matched datasets enable reliable MIA evaluation without distribution-shift artifacts. We benchmark multiple MIA methods and conduct modality disentanglement experiments on these datasets. The results reveal that LALM memorization is cross-modal, arising only from binding a speaker's vocal identity with its text. These findings establish a principled standard for auditing LALMs beyond spurious correlations.

Method

Limited data exposure during LALM training makes verbatim memorization unlikely, yet membership can still leak through memorized audio–text associations. To measure this reliably, we adopt a three-phase privacy-auditing framework that first removes distributional confounds, then audits the model, and finally characterizes what it memorizes.

Phase 1 — Multi-Modal Blind Baseline. Without accessing the target model, we train logistic-regression classifiers on data-intrinsic features at three levels of modality coverage: metadata (duration, file size, word/character counts), text (utterance-level TF-IDF over unigrams and bigrams), and acoustic descriptors (MFCCs, spectral statistics, pitch, energy, zero-crossing rate). A high blind-baseline AUC, together with a positive correlation to the model's own MIA scores, flags leakage that is driven by distribution shift rather than genuine memorization.

Phase 2 — MIA Auditing. On the distribution-matched (clean) datasets, we run a gray-box two-stage generation protocol: the target model greedily decodes a self-generated transcript, then re-runs a forward pass to obtain token-level logits for that transcript. From these self-conditioned probabilities we compute complementary membership indicators — perplexity, Shannon entropy, Min-k%/Max-k% probability, Max-Rényi divergence, zlib ratio, and max probability gap — and aggregate them with a logistic-regression classifier under stratified cross-validation.

Phase 3 — Modality Disentanglement. To test what the model actually memorizes, we keep the text but replace or perturb the audio (zero tensor, noise, or TTS/TTA re-synthesis). Membership signal survives only when the original audio is paired with its text; every audio substitution collapses the AUC toward chance. Memorization is therefore cross-modal — it comes from binding a speaker's vocal identity to the corresponding text.

Key Findings

  • Distribution shift inflates MIA. Common audio benchmarks show near-perfect train/test separability (AUC ≈ 1.0) from blind baselines alone, before any model inference — much reported leakage reflects curation and preprocessing artifacts, not memorization.
  • Reliable evaluation needs matched distributions. On distribution-matched datasets the genuine membership signal is modest (AUC ≈ 0.62), giving a far more cautious picture of privacy risk.
  • Memorization is cross-modal. Replacing the original audio with a zero tensor, noise, or re-synthesized speech drops the AUC to about 0.5; leakage appears only when the real audio is paired with its text.
  • Risk concentrates on the speaker. Privacy risk is highest when sensitive content is tightly linked to a particular speaker's vocal identity.

Models & Datasets

We audit two state-of-the-art, fully open large audio language models whose training data is documented and whose ground-truth membership labels are available — Audio Flamingo 3 (AF3) and Music Flamingo (MF3) — across eight audio datasets spanning ASR, audio captioning, and music understanding.

The curated member/non-member splits are released as the MIA_LALM dataset, covering clotho, cochlscene, gigaspeech, librispeech, nsynth, spgispeech, tedlium, and voxpopuli. The attack pipeline, blind baselines, and our own released attack outputs are available in the code repository.
